# Telotia — Voice & Vocabulary

How Telotia's public copy sounds, and — more importantly — *which words it uses*. This governs all site copy. Pairs with [SITE_DIRECTION.md](./SITE_DIRECTION.md).

> Public brand name is **Telotia** only. Internal names (Norma, Principia, Vivarium, Limen) never appear in public copy.

## Who we're talking to

The person who **does CAPA / quality investigations and loses hours hunting for the evidence** behind each statement before an audit. Secondary readers: Centech evaluators and the research community. Everyone is sober, technical, and allergic to marketing — write for them.

Their pain, in their words: *"I know the conclusion is right; proving it — finding the page, the record, the citation for every line — is what eats the week."* Speak to that, not to our product category.

## The core rule: their language on the surface, our ontology underneath

Our internal model grades claims **supported / partial / contradicted / unsupported**. That is *our ontology, not their language.* Two traps:

1. A quality engineer doesn't think in those four words — they think in **objective evidence** (the auditor's term), gaps, and findings. Making them learn our vocabulary in the first five seconds is a tax on the person we most want to feel understood.
2. **"Claim" collides.** In medical devices, *claims* means product/labeling claims. "Claim validation" can read as marketing-claims substantiation. On the surface, say **statement**, not claim.

So we layer the vocabulary:

| Layer | Vocabulary | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Surface** | plain, self-evident evidence-status; "statement" not "claim" | homepage first screen, ledger chips |
| **Product / formal** | the four graded verdicts, defined and mapped 1:1 | walkthrough detail, glossary, Research page |

Both are honest. The surface is what they already say; the formal layer is available one level deeper for the sophisticated reader (and the "graded, not pass/fail" idea — real differentiation — lives there).

## Surface translation (use these on the homepage)

Under a column header of **EVIDENCE**, the chips answer "where's the evidence?":

| Internal verdict | Surface chip |
|---|---|
| supported | **Found** |
| partial | **Incomplete** |
| contradicted | **Conflicting** |
| unsupported | **None on file** |

Why this is *more* honest, not less: the internal label "Unsupported" needs the caveat *"silent, not wrong"* to avoid misleading. **"None on file" is self-caveating** — the label *is* the caveat. Self-evident beats defined (brand rule).

## Words to avoid

- **Internal names** — Norma, Principia, Vivarium, Limen. Public name is Telotia only.
- **Their authority words** — "compliant," "audit-passed," "verified," "certified," "validated." Verification/validation are terms of art in device-land; a "Verified" chip overclaims. Describe what the software *did* — found / checked / cited — never what it *guarantees*.
- **Unearned claims** (brand rule) — "customers," "design/pilot partner," "incorporated," "IP secured," "trusted by," employer names, "in Rust," superlatives, any metric we can't cite.
- **Forward promises stated as fact** — a launch date, an SLA, a benchmark result — unless it's real.

## Tense discipline (the whole game)

Nothing is running yet. Copy is **building / designed / intended**, never **does / has / offers**. A sober evaluator reads for exactly this. No fabricated run-counts, timestamps, or "live" badges — a mockup is labeled *"illustrative — the workflow we're building."*

## Tone

- Restraint is the brand. Quiet confidence, not marketing energy — but *chosen* restraint, not timid.
- Human-in-the-loop is a promise, not a caveat: **"You still sign off; you just stop hunting for page numbers."**
- Self-evident > defined. If a sentence isn't self-evident, demonstrated, or cited — cut it.

## Hero copy (approved options)

H1 (pick one):
- **"Every statement in your CAPA, checked against the evidence."** — concrete, names their document.
- **"Objective evidence for every statement — before the auditor asks."** — their term of art + the stakes.

Sub: *"Telotia reads the record, finds what backs each statement, and shows the gaps — with citations your auditor can follow. You still sign off; you just stop hunting for page numbers."*

Kicker: `Paid pilot · Montreal` (no launch date until one is real).

The mission line — *"Every important artifact makes claims. We validate which claims survive evidence."* — is strong but abstract; it lives as the **Research page masthead**, not the homepage H1.
