01China manufacturing expands again, but orders and hiring lag

Macro | Released September 30 at 09:30 Beijing time; reference month: September 2026.

Facts
The official manufacturing PMI rose from 49.8 to 50.1. Production reached 51.7, while new orders eased from 50.6 to 50.5 and employment stood at 48.4. Medium and small firms remained below 50. The threshold denotes expansion versus contraction, not output growth.

Market impact | Inference
Recovering production may improve expectations for industrial and materials demand. Slower order momentum, however, provides less support for a broad earnings recovery.

Countercase | Scenarios
If activity mainly reflects the removal of disruptions rather than final demand, inventories and pricing pressure could follow. Conversely, sustained expansion in orders would challenge an overly cautious view.

02Australian CPI reaches 4%, yet a softer surprise eases near-term hike bets

Central banks / FX | Released September 30 at 11:30 AEST; reference month: August 2026.

Facts
Annual CPI rose from 3.5% to 4.0%; trimmed mean inflation held at 3.6%. Monthly CPI increased 0.4% unadjusted and 0.7% seasonally adjusted. Reuters and ABC reported a slightly softer-than-expected release; Reuters reported declines in the Australian dollar and three-year yields.

Market impact | Inference
A higher inflation level can coexist with lower near-term rate expectations. For housing, consumer spending and financing-sensitive businesses, persistent underlying inflation matters more than the headline alone.

Countercase | Scenarios
Underlying inflation remains above the 2%–3% target band; further energy pass-through could require tightening. Conversely, lower energy costs and weaker demand could make extrapolating headline inflation too pessimistic.

  • Reuters: Australian inflation and currency reaction (Sep 30; independent reporting, syndicated)

03Treasuries find a morning respite; monthly dollar strength still matters

Rates / FX | Early European trading, September 30; Reuters update at 04:59 EDT, not a closing snapshot.

Facts
Reuters reported the US 10-year yield at 5.209% and the two-year at 4.870%, both lower on the day. The dollar eased slightly but remained on course for roughly a 2% monthly gain. Prior-day Dow Jones reporting and bank research also documented elevated long yields.

Market impact | Inference
Falling yields offer temporary valuation relief without establishing a reversal in financing costs. A firm dollar can increase debt burdens for some overseas borrowers and reduce translated foreign revenue for US multinationals.

Countercase | Scenarios
Weaker growth or inflation could extend the bond rebound and weaken the dollar. Renewed energy pressure or a higher term premium could reverse the respite. Policy expectations do not explain every yield move.
