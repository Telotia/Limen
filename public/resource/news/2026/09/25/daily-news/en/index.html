01 / 06

US long bonds sell off while equities hold their ground

Rates / macro | September 24, New York afternoon; September 25 Asian context

Fact

The US 10-year Treasury yield reached 5.196% in Thursday’s New York afternoon report; the 30-year yield hit a more-than-20-year high. A second agency rounded the 10-year reading to 5.20% in its closing review. The broad US large-cap and technology composite indexes each closed less than 0.1% changed.

Market impact | Inference

Higher risk-free yields raise financing and valuation hurdles. Equity resilience may reflect earnings support, but does not establish that bond pressure has disappeared.

Countercase | Scenario

Cooling inflation expectations or safe-haven bond demand could lower yields. Sustained profit growth could also help equities absorb higher discount rates. These transmission channels do not prove same-day causation.

02 / 06

Mexico holds at 6.50% and opens up its forward guidance

Macro / central banks | September 24 decision, included in today’s briefing

Fact

Mexico’s central bank unanimously held its policy rate at 6.50% for a third meeting. It removed explicit guidance favouring continued unchanged rates. It still expects headline inflation to reach 3% in Q4 2027 and says policy need not mechanically follow the United States.

Market impact | Inference

The wording leaves more policy flexibility and may affect rate differentials and short bonds. It is not a promise to cut. Weak growth and inflation risks need joint assessment.

Countercase | Scenario

The bank still sees inflation risks tilted upward. Energy and currency pass-through could prevent easing; weaker demand would instead raise the growth cost of maintaining restrictive policy.

03 / 06

Yen rebounds as official currency comments draw attention

FX | September 25, 03:54 UTC news; 06:15 UTC market report

Fact

Japan’s finance authorities said US-Japan leaders discussed yen weakness. A subsequent Asian-session wire report put the dollar down 0.4% at 158.23 yen. The dollar overall remained on course for a weekly gain of about 1%; the week was not yet complete.

Market impact | Inference

Official communication may increase the risk premium on short-yen positions, offsetting pressure from high US yields. A stronger yen generally reduces import costs but can lower translated export earnings.

Countercase | Scenario

This is the Japanese account of the talks, not evidence of executed intervention or an agreed joint operation. Persistent US rate advantages could limit the rebound.

04 / 06

Oil eases early, but talks have not restored supply

Energy / industry | September 25, 00:32 UTC prices; September 24, 20:32 EDT

Fact

The cited wire snapshot put Brent futures down 0.69% at $105.85 a barrel and WTI down 0.86% at $93.80, after respective Thursday gains of 3.4% and 2.7%. It described exploration of a phased US-Iran arrangement, not a completed reopening of the strait.

Market impact | Inference

Lower oil can relieve airline, transport and chemical costs; sustained relief needs actual shipments and safe passage. Regional supply conditions and contract conventions differ, so the price gap is not an assured arbitrage return.

Countercase | Scenario

Further attacks or failed talks could rebuild the risk premium; restored supply could lower prices further. Other reports use differing contract quotations. This item retains the paired prices and percentage moves from one timestamped report.

05 / 06

Japanese stocks rise for a fifth session, led by banks and chips

Equities / industry | September 25 close; 03:03 EDT wire report

Fact

Japan’s main 225-stock index closed up 1.30% and its broader index rose 1.31%. The banking sector gained 4.08%. The wire cited AI-related shares and buying ahead of the mid-term dividend eligibility deadline. These are closing figures, distinct from the earlier gain of about 1.2%.

Market impact | Inference

Bank participation broadens the day’s support beyond technology. Dividend-related buying also reflects the calendar, however, and does not independently establish improved earnings prospects.

Countercase | Scenario

Ex-dividend price adjustments mean dividends are not risk-free extra returns. Higher yields can support bank margins while increasing bond valuation losses and borrower credit risk.

06 / 06

US membership retailer grows, with a refund benefit to separate

Company / consumption | September 24 after the close; 16 weeks ended August 30

Fact

The issuer reported quarterly net sales of $93.9 billion, up 11.2%, and comparable sales growth of 6.7% excluding gasoline prices and FX. Diluted EPS of $6.75 included a $0.15 net tariff-refund benefit; simple subtraction gives $6.60. Net sales should not be confused with total revenue including membership fees.

Market impact | Inference

Comparable sales indicate resilience in this business model. Separating one-time benefits improves assessment of recurring earnings. One retailer cannot represent all consumers.

Countercase | Scenario

Gasoline and FX inflated nominal sales, and refunds may not recur. Wages, logistics and member retention can affect margins. Strong earnings already reflected in valuation need not lift the share price.
