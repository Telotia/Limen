A Policy Split: The Fed Hikes for the First Time in Three Years While the Bank of England Waits

Strong U.S. consumption and elevated inflation lifted front-end rates. The Bank of England is still testing whether the energy shock will spread. The dollar, the yield curve, oil and AI infrastructure repriced around that divide.

MAIN THREAD · EDITORIAL VIEW

Confirmed facts: the Federal Reserve unanimously raised rates by 25 basis points to 3.75%–4.00% and projected another increase this year; U.S. August retail sales rose 1.2%; the Bank of England voted 6–3 to hold at 3.75%. Editorial view: the market question has shifted from whether central banks will tighten to how much real financing cost resilient demand can absorb, and for how long.

01
Macro / Central banks / United States

The Fed unanimously hikes 25 basis points and lifts its 2026 rate median to 4.1%

FACTS

On September 16, the Federal Reserve voted 12–0 to raise the federal funds target range to 3.75%–4.00%, its first increase in three years. The September projections raised the median end-2026 policy rate to 4.1% from 3.8% in June, real GDP growth to 2.3% from 2.2%, and PCE inflation to 3.7% from 3.6%, while lowering unemployment to 4.1% from 4.3%. Sixteen participants projected at least one more quarter-point increase this year.

MARKET IMPACT · INFERENCE

the unanimous vote plus stronger growth and inflation projections make this more than an isolated insurance move. Front-end yields and the dollar face a higher policy path, while funding-dependent and long-duration assets face a stricter valuation threshold.

COUNTER-RISK

The projections are a distribution of individual judgments, not a promise. Lower energy prices or a sudden labor-market slowdown could change the path. Long yields could also fall if stronger inflation credibility reduces term inflation risk.

02
Macro / Consumption / United States

U.S. August retail sales rebound 1.2%, supporting both growth and the case for tighter policy

FACTS

The U.S. Census Bureau estimated August retail and food-services sales at $773.9 billion, up 1.2% from July after a revised 0.5% decline. Sales excluding gasoline stations still rose 1.1%. The control group used in GDP calculations climbed 1.4%, its largest increase since September 2024. The series is not adjusted for inflation, so the nominal gain combines prices and volumes.

MARKET IMPACT · INFERENCE

consumption did not immediately buckle under expensive energy and high rates, giving the Fed more room to prioritize inflation. Strong nominal sales, however, do not establish an equal improvement in household purchasing power.

COUNTER-RISK

Online promotion timing, back-to-school demand and gasoline prices amplified the monthly rebound. The data can reverse, while higher credit costs and weak confidence may still weigh on discretionary demand.

Rates / FX / Equities

The dollar reaches a seven-week high as the curve bear-flattens and U.S. futures rebound

FACTS

Near 02:00 EDT on September 17, the dollar index traded around 100.3, close to its strongest level since July 31; the euro was near $1.1463 and the dollar near 155.98 yen. The two-year Treasury yield was about 4.7153%, its highest since 2024, while the 10-year yield was just below 5%. On September 16, the S&P 500 fell 0.4%, the Dow lost 1.2% and the Nasdaq was nearly flat. In early European trading Thursday, S&P 500 and Dow futures rose about 0.8% and 0.7%.

MARKET IMPACT · INFERENCE

a larger rise at the front end shows that markets moved tightening into the near term while allowing credible inflation action to cap longer-run inflation risk. The technology-futures rebound looks like relief from resolved event risk, not proof that the high discount-rate constraint has disappeared.

COUNTER-RISK

All quotes are time-specific and may reverse after the cash open. More upside U.S. data could pull long yields higher; lower oil and safe-haven demand could instead support long bonds.

Macro / Central banks / United Kingdom

The Bank of England holds at 3.75% by a 6–3 vote as three members still seek an immediate hike

FACTS

The Bank of England voted 6–3 to keep Bank Rate at 3.75% in September; three members preferred a 25-basis-point increase to 4.00%. U.K. CPI inflation rose to 3.1% in August from 2.9% in July, above the 2% target. The Bank said recent energy developments could keep inflation above target for longer, while the majority continued to weigh domestic costs and a softer labor market.

MARKET IMPACT · INFERENCE

the United Kingdom chose to wait while preserving the option to act, creating a timing split with the Fed. Sterling and front-end gilts will depend more heavily on whether energy bills generate second-round effects in wages and services.

COUNTER-RISK

The 6–3 split was unchanged from July and did not show an expanding hike bloc. Lower energy and weak demand could prevent a hike; renewed wage growth or inflation expectations could quickly narrow the waiting window.

05
Industry / Energy / Shipping

Red Sea diplomacy pulls Brent toward $104.60, but physical-flow risks remain

FACTS

AP recorded Brent crude down 1.2% at $104.60 a barrel in early European trading on September 17, still far above roughly $72 in late February. Axios cited a regional source saying U.S. diplomats met Houthi representatives in Oman over the weekend to discuss preserving a ceasefire and navigation through Bab el-Mandeb; the U.S. vice president later confirmed direct conversations. Hormuz traffic remains constrained and a key Saudi pipeline remains under repair.

MARKET IMPACT · INFERENCE

diplomatic contact removed part of the Red Sea shipping premium, but crude, refined-product, insurance and rerouting costs need actual traffic normalization for a durable decline. Cooler energy also gives marginal support to long bonds and duration-sensitive equities.

COUNTER-RISK

The meeting details rely on sourced reporting, and the State Department did not confirm the specific session. Houthi-Saudi fighting continues. A new attack, repair delay or worse Hormuz disruption could reverse the oil decline quickly.

Industry / Company / AI infrastructure

Huawei launches the Ascend 960 supernode as AI competition moves from chips to systems

FACTS

Huawei introduced its Ascend 960 supernode in Shanghai on September 17, saying the system uses a new NPO interconnect and targets very large model training and inference. AP described it as a faster successor to a system launched only months earlier; the company also outlined Ascend 970 and 980 series plans for 2028 and 2029. Public materials frame the product around accelerators, memory and interconnect operating as one system.

MARKET IMPACT · INFERENCE

under advanced-chip constraints, scale-out architecture, networking and software coordination become central ways to offset gaps at the individual-chip level. The investable supply chain therefore broadens to optics, networking, memory, power and cooling.

COUNTER-RISK

Launch specifications and vendor performance claims still require independent benchmarks, manufacturing yields, software compatibility and customer deployments. Larger systems also increase power use, network complexity and capital spending.
