Markets find relief; inflation expectations remain a constraint

Weekend briefing · Sentiment, yields and contract delivery

September 26, 2026 · Cutoff 07:00 EDT (Toronto, UTC−4)

It is Saturday; US regular equity trading is closed. This edition uses the latest session on September 25 and public information verifiable by today’s cutoff. September 24 disclosures retain their original dates. Editorial view: cheaper oil and corporate orders offer near-term support; inflation, employment and cash generation will test its durability.

01Consumers grow more worried about prices

Macro | Released September 25; final September survey

Fact: Consumer sentiment fell to 48.1 from 51.7 in August, although it improved from the preliminary 47.8. One-year inflation expectations rose from 4.0% to 4.6%; long-run expectations increased from 3.3% to 3.4%. These are survey expectations, not measured inflation.

Market impact: Inference: Purchasing-power concerns increase demand risk for consumer businesses. Higher inflation expectations also complicate any policy response to weaker confidence.

Countercase / risk: Countercase: Sentiment is not actual spending. Employment, wages or cheaper energy could support purchases; the upward revision also qualifies the weakest headlines.

02Short yields ease; long bonds and the yen diverge

Rates / FX | September 25 official daily curve; FX report at 07:50 EDT

Fact: Treasury daily par yields were 4.81% for two years, 5.17% for ten years and 5.49% for thirty years, versus 4.87%, 5.18% and 5.47% a day earlier. A morning FX report placed the yen about 0.8% stronger at 157.65 per dollar. That is an intraday snapshot, not a New York close.

Market impact: Inference: Lower short yields do not imply broad relief in long-term funding costs. Yen strength can alter carry-trade incentives and ease Japanese import-price pressure.

Countercase / risk: Countercase: One session cannot establish a policy turn. Safe-haven demand, bond supply and official currency communication can move prices; a stronger yen is not evidence of actual intervention.

03US stocks rebound as AI and earnings offer support

Equities | September 25 US regular-session close

Fact: All three major US benchmarks rose Friday: the S&P 500 by about 0.5%, the Dow by about 0.9% and the Nasdaq Composite by about 0.5%. Agency reporting highlighted AI and technology support; all three also posted weekly gains. Rounded returns are used because reported closing index levels differ slightly across outlets.

Market impact: Inference: Cheaper oil and earnings expectations offer some valuation support. Index gains do not establish equally strong participation across companies or sectors.

Countercase / risk: Countercase: Persistent rate pressure can outweigh profit growth. Conversely, delivered earnings and cheaper energy could extend the advance; high yields alone do not establish an imminent decline.

04Oil retreats; talks are not restored shipping

Energy | September 25 trading and diplomatic reports

Fact: Two news agencies reported lower oil prices Friday. One cited sources close to talks describing a possible phased US–Iran de-escalation, while supply and shipping risks persisted. Conflicting absolute Brent quotes remain unresolved, so this edition reports direction only. Negotiation reports are not a signed agreement or confirmed reopening.

Market impact: Inference: Sustained physical-flow recovery could reduce energy risk premiums and imported inflation, potentially helping transport and consumer businesses. This remains a conditional scenario.

Countercase / risk: Countercase: Talks may reverse. Attacks, insurance constraints and logistics bottlenecks can offset diplomatic progress. A one-day price decline cannot establish normalized supply.

05A compute contract lifts shares; cash spending comes first

Company / AI infrastructure | September 24 disclosure; September 25 market reaction

Fact: AKAM disclosed a seven-year, $11.6 billion commitment from an AI developer. Related capital spending is estimated at $5.5 billion, including about $1.7 billion of additional 2026 spending; 2026 revenue guidance is unchanged. Filings make payments conditional on delivery, service availability and termination provisions. Shares closed about 3.2% higher Friday; this is distinct from the previous after-hours jump.

Market impact: Inference: AI demand extends into CPUs, memory and cloud infrastructure. Total contract value is not current revenue or free cash flow.

Countercase / risk: Countercase: Customer concentration, delivery and financing costs can constrain returns; attached warrants may dilute shareholders. Successful delivery and utilization could improve longer-term revenue visibility.

06Stablecoin rules move into detail, pending consultation

Financial industry / regulation | September 24 proposals; September 25 coverage

Fact: The Federal Reserve proposed two rules for payment stablecoin issuers under its supervision: full backing with permissible reserve assets, capital and risk-management standards, and an application process for banks. Comments close 60 days after Federal Register publication. These are proposals, not final rules in force or a blanket endorsement of digital assets.

Market impact: Inference: Greater clarity could support compliant payments, custody and short-term Treasury services. New business depends on final rules, approvals and adoption.

Countercase / risk: Countercase: Compliance costs, redemption pressure and operational failures may offset growth. Full backing does not eliminate liquidity or service risk, and proposals can change.
