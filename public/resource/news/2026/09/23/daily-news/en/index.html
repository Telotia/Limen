1Eurozone and Indian PMIs rebound, with costs still in focus

Macro | September flash surveys released September 23

Fact

The eurozone composite PMI rose from 52.0 to 53.1, beating the Reuters poll forecast of 51.7, while operating costs accelerated. India’s composite rose from 54.3 to 56.5; Reuters also reported weaker export momentum and subdued hiring. These are preliminary surveys, with 50 separating expansion from contraction.

Market impact · Inference

Improving demand could support cyclical orders, but higher costs may limit profit conversion. Survey expansion is not a measured GDP growth rate, and stronger activity alone does not imply monetary easing.

Countercase · Scenario

Flash readings may be revised and energy effects may arrive with a lag. Weak exports and hiring could prevent a one-month recovery from becoming sustained growth.

2Brent falls below $100; a pipeline restart still needs delivery proof

Energy / industry | September 23, 06:51 UTC prices; September 22 supply reports

Fact

Reuters quoted Brent futures at $98.47 a barrel, down 0.79%, and WTI at $89.31, down 1.34%. Sources had reported a restart of Saudi Arabia’s East-West Pipeline at reduced flows. Resumed loading and full operating capacity are separate milestones.

Market impact · Inference

Sustained physical supply recovery could ease imported inflation, transport costs and working-capital pressure. Lower oil prices could also reduce upstream producers’ revenue; energy shares need not track the broader market.

Countercase · Scenario

The reports include anonymous sourcing, and full restoration remains uncertain. Failed diplomacy or renewed transport disruption could restore the risk premium. These are futures snapshots, not spot or closing prices.

3Dollar holds near a two-month high as rate support persists

Rates / FX | September 23 Asian morning; Australian decision next week

Fact

The early snapshot in Reuters’ 01:43 UTC report put the dollar index at 100.56 and EUR/USD at 1.1446, linking dollar resilience to further tightening expectations. A separate Asian market report quoted USD/JPY at 157.60. Australia’s official calendar confirms its next decision on September 29 at 14:30 AEST.

Market impact · Inference

Sticky inflation could sustain dollar interest-rate support and raise the burden of some emerging-market dollar debts. Oil and the dollar need not move together; cheaper oil alone does not establish a weaker-dollar case.

Countercase · Scenario

Sustained energy relief could reduce tightening expectations, while Japanese policy or intervention risk could alter the yen’s path. Expectations are not central-bank commitments, and the currency snapshots are not simultaneous.

4Korean chips lift the close; European opening gains remain selective

Equities / sectors | Korean September 23 close; Europe at 07:08 UTC

Fact

The KOSPI closed up 0.90% at 7,080.92 despite foreign net selling. Samsung Electronics gained 3.25% and SK Hynix 1.20%. Europe’s STOXX 600 was up 0.4% at 645.04 at 07:08 UTC, led by aerospace and defence. That European opening snapshot predates the eurozone PMI release.

Market impact · Inference

Chip earnings expectations and lower energy costs could support risk appetite. Yet sector leadership alongside foreign selling means index gains do not demonstrate broad buying participation.

Countercase · Scenario

Profit-taking and elevated discount rates could amplify volatility. Korean markets close September 24–25 for holidays, potentially accumulating overseas news for reopening. Europe’s opening move is not a full-day result.

5Seed producer’s lower sales put cash flow and profit quality in focus

Company / agriculture | FY2025/26 results released September 23

Fact

KWS reported roughly €1.63 billion in annual sales, down 3%, an adjusted EBITDA margin of 19.3% versus 21.1% including a one-off effect, and €122.5 million of free cash flow. Reuters’ European opening report put the shares down 6.1%, citing reduced sugarbeet and corn acreage as a sales drag.

Market impact · Inference

Agricultural input demand follows acreage and farm budgets and may diverge from technology trading. Comparing adjusted and reported margins with cash flow helps assess the quality of operating improvements.

Countercase · Scenario

Lower sales do not necessarily imply worsening financial stress: cash flow remains positive. Conversely, a one-off gain does not establish underlying earnings growth. The opening share move is not the close.
