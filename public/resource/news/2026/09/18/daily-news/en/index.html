DAILY FINANCE · SEPTEMBER 18, 2026

Japan Hikes, the Yen Falls: A Credibility Trade

The Bank of Japan lifted its policy rate to a 31-year high, yet the yen weakened. UK consumption beat expectations, while lower oil futures still contrasted with constrained physical flows.

Information cutoff: September 18, 2026, 07:20 EDT / 11:20 UTC

Main thread | Editorial view: The BOJ voted 7–2 to raise its policy rate from 1.00% to 1.25%, while the dollar gained as much as about 1% to 157.54 yen. August core CPI rose 1.7%; the measure excluding fresh food and energy rose 1.9%. Size, dissent and guidance now matter more than one move.
01 · Macro / Central banks / Japan

BOJ raises rates to 1.25% as core inflation undershoots

Fact

The overnight-rate target rose from around 1.00% to 1.25%, the highest since 1995. August core CPI rose 1.7%, below the 1.8% survey median and July reading; CPI excluding fresh food and energy rose 1.9%.

Market impact | Inference

The interval between hikes shortened to three months, signaling a more pre-emptive response to energy, imports and yen weakness.

Counter-risk

Two members dissented and core CPI undershot. Softer energy or growth could delay the next move.

02 · Rates / FX / Equities

Yen falls after the hike while the Nikkei rises

Fact

The dollar gained up to 1% to 157.54 yen. Japan's two-year yield fell about four basis points to 1.82%; the Nikkei 225 gained 1.4% to 65,018.95. European shares fell 0.3%, US futures rose 0.3%–0.6%, and the US 10-year yield was near 4.93%.

Market impact | Inference

Dissent and cautious guidance reduced confidence in rapid follow-up tightening. Yen weakness can lift import inflation and pressure the BOJ to act again.

Counter-risk

Repatriation or verbal intervention could reverse the currency; equities may only reflect an exporter benefit.

03 · Macro / Consumption / UK

UK retail sales rise 0.5%; sterling edges higher

Fact

August retail volumes rose 0.5% versus a 0.2% decline expected, gained 2.4% year over year and rose 0.9% over three months. Sterling added 0.1% to about $1.337. The Bank of England held at 3.75% and sees inflation slightly above 4% in early 2027.

Market impact | Inference

Consumption resilience weakens the case that growth is too fragile for a hike. Markets put the November hike probability near 65%.

Counter-risk

Weather and promotions distort monthly data; energy and mortgage costs may weaken spending.

  4. Bank of England
04 · Energy / Shipping

Brent falls for a third day; physical traffic stays weak

Fact

At 10:08 UTC, Brent fell 1.6% to $103.17 and was down 1.4% for the week. Only four commodity vessels crossed Hormuz on Thursday versus a 10-day average near 16. Iran claimed a tanker strike that AP could not verify independently.

Market impact | Inference

Futures price alternative loading and inventories, while some physical prices around $120, insurance and shipping risk show flows have not normalized.

Counter-risk

Transponder data and pipeline repair estimates are uncertain. New attacks could rebuild the premium quickly.

05 · Companies / Semiconductors

Solidigm weighs a US NAND fab; SK Hynix gains 6.4%

Fact

Reuters cited three sources saying Solidigm is evaluating a US NAND fab, with upstate New York a leading candidate, separate from Ohio talks with Intel. SK Hynix said no plan was confirmed. Its shares rose 6.4% versus a 2.7% KOSPI gain.

Market impact | Inference

US capacity could reduce reliance on Dalian and cushion tariffs or equipment restrictions. AI storage demand turns localization into a valuation catalyst.

Counter-risk

There is no final agreement, budget or production date; US costs are higher and demand may slow.

06 · Companies / Robotics

Toyota automation could cost ¥1 trillion annually

Fact

Toyota estimates group and supplier factory modernization could cost about ¥1 trillion annually from 2028 and involve about 400,000 replacement and new robots. It did not confirm the spending or duration. Shares fell about 0.3%.

Market impact | Inference

Ageing plants, labor shortages and physical AI broaden automotive capital spending into robots, sensors, logistics and software.

Counter-risk

This is an estimate, not a fixed budget, and the robot count includes replacements.
