2026-08-03 | Cutoff: information visible around the North American pre-open; quotes are reported-time snapshots

Main theme: Joint U.S.-Japan intervention, easing Iran tensions and a sharp Korean chip reversal pulled markets from “AI earnings validation” toward policy credibility, geopolitical risk and high-volatility pricing. Facts, inferences and counter-risks are separated. Not investment advice.

1. U.S.-Japan intervention sends the yen sharply higher

Fact: The U.S. and Japan confirmed joint intervention last week. The dollar briefly fell from near 164 yen to about 155.20 and was around 156.68 late in Tokyo. AP reported that the U.S. Treasury bought yen through the New York Fed.

Market impact: Yen-funded carry trades face repricing; a stronger yen lowers Japan’s import costs but reduces the yen value of overseas earnings.

Counter-risk: If rate differentials, energy imports and capital flows remain unchanged, the exchange rate may retest the weak-yen range.

AP global markets · AP intervention details · Japan MOF

2. Iran de-escalation pulls oil lower, but geopolitical premium remains

Fact: President Trump said the U.S. would hold off on new attacks and that a deal was near. Brent fell 4.7% to $83.92 and U.S. crude fell 5.6% to $79.89.

Market impact: Lower oil temporarily eases inflation, transport and consumer-cost pressure and supports risk assets.

Counter-risk: This is political guidance, not a signed agreement; a breakdown in talks or transport disruption could push inflation expectations and long yields higher.

AP · EIA · IEA

3. Asian equities reprice chip risk: Kospi falls 5.1%

Fact: South Korea’s Kospi fell 5.1% on Aug. 3 after a record one-day gain in the prior session. Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix each fell 8.8%; Taiwan’s Taiex rose 0.6%.

Market impact: The AI complex is showing a high-beta “strong earnings, weak price” pattern, with positioning, leverage and valuation amplifying fundamentals.

Counter-risk: One day does not prove an AI-demand reversal; sustained HBM/data-center orders could make this deleveraging, while a capex or memory-price downgrade would magnify downside.

AP · AP Samsung · Samsung IR · SK Hynix IR

4. Amazon is a positive AI-payback example, but cash flow remains a constraint

Fact: AP reported Amazon rose 15.3% on Friday; profit more than tripled year over year as cloud growth accelerated. Axios reported Q2 sales of $200.6 billion and AWS growth of 37%, while heavy AI investment pushed free cash flow negative.

Market impact: Investors are rewarding companies that turn AI infrastructure demand into cloud revenue and profit, shifting the debate toward returns rather than capex alone.

Counter-risk: AWS growth, margins and capex payback need several quarters of confirmation; excess supply or higher power/chip costs could reverse the re-rating.

AP · Amazon IR · Axios · Quarterly results

5. Fed holds, but three officials favored a hike

Fact: The Fed kept its policy rate around 3.6%, its fifth consecutive hold. Hammack, Kashkari and Logan favored a hike. AP reported the 10-year Treasury near 4.64% before the decision, versus about 4.50% in mid-June.

Market impact: “No hike” does not mean rapid easing; dissents and elevated inflation keep discount-rate, dollar and long-bond volatility high.

Counter-risk: Three votes are not a majority; a material labor-market deterioration could quickly revive rate-cut pricing.

AP · Fed decision · FOMC calendar

6. Data relay: manufacturing, job openings and payrolls

Fact: The New York Fed calendar lists ISM Manufacturing for Aug. 3; JOLTS, trade and factory orders for Aug. 4; ADP and ISM Services for Aug. 5; and the Employment Situation for Aug. 7 at 8:30 a.m. ET. The FRED/BLS calendar also lists Aug. 7.

Market impact (inference): These releases will test the “slowing but not recessionary” narrative. Strong labor data could support yields and the dollar; weak data could reinforce easing bets.

Counter-risk: Monthly noise, seasonal adjustment, revisions, oil and tariffs could produce weaker growth and higher inflation together.

New York Fed · FRED/BLS · BLS · ISM
