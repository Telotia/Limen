Weak Consumption Meets Rebounding Oil

U.S. spending data cooled, but crude oil and long-term yields rose together. Markets face a growth-inflation bind, not a simple “bad news is good for rates” setup.

01 · MACRO

Retail sales unexpectedly fell; the control group also declined

Fact
July retail and food-services sales were $763.6 billion, down 0.6% monthly but up 5.0% yearly. Sales excluding gasoline and auto dealers fell 0.2%; the GDP control group fell 0.4%. Autos fell 1.8%, online sales 2.2%, while restaurants gained 0.5%.

Market impact · Inference
First-half boosts from tax refunds, the World Cup, and earlier promotions are fading, putting third-quarter consumption forecasts at risk. Higher energy costs could deepen the real-income squeeze.

Counter-risk
Timing effects and auto normalization explain part of the decline. Retail data omit most services, and one month does not establish a consumer recession.

Sources: U.S. Census Bureau · AP · FRED · Axios

02 · MACRO

Sentiment fell to 51.0 while inflation expectations stayed high

Fact
Preliminary University of Michigan sentiment fell from 55.2 to 51.0. One-year inflation expectations rose to 4.3%; five-year expectations held at 3.3%.

Market impact · Inference
Hard and soft data now agree on slower consumption, but sticky expectations leave policy with weaker growth and still-high inflation.

Counter-risk
The reading will be revised on August 28. Confidence and spending do not move one-for-one, while wealth effects may support higher-income households.

Sources: University of Michigan · Axios · AP · FRED

03 · RATES / ENERGY / FX

Brent rebounded 1.7%; the 10-year yield rose to 4.69%

Fact
Brent closed at $88.52 a barrel. The 10-year Treasury yield rose from 4.63% to 4.69%. July CPI was 3.4% year over year, still above the 2.4% pace before the Iran war.

Market impact · Inference
Yields followed oil higher instead of weak consumption lower, showing energy inflation and term premium dominated. Dollar support from rates would tighten global conditions.

Counter-risk
Improved Gulf shipping could reverse oil and let weak demand regain control of bonds. One session does not confirm a trend.

Sources: AP · U.S. Treasury · Federal Reserve H.15 · U.S. EIA

04 · EQUITIES

Stocks eased from records while small caps outperformed

Fact
The S&P 500 fell 0.2% to 7,785.76, the Dow lost 0.2%, and the Nasdaq declined 0.3%; the Russell 2000 gained 0.5%. The S&P still completed a third weekly gain. South Korea's Kospi rose 2.4%.

Market impact · Inference
A shallow retreat and small-cap strength look more like internal rotation than wholesale risk reduction.

Counter-risk
Positioning may be crowded after three winning weeks. Higher oil or yields could turn rotation into broad multiple compression.

Sources: AP close · AP market report · AP record-close recap · Official S&P 500 page

05 · INDUSTRY / COMPANY

Applied Materials posted records but fell 5.1%

Fact
Fiscal Q3 revenue was $9.115 billion, up 25%; GAAP EPS was $3.17. Fiscal Q4 guidance centers on $10.25 billion of revenue and $4.02 of non-GAAP EPS. Shares fell 5.1% Friday.

Market impact · Inference
AI equipment demand remains strong, but the valuation hurdle now requires repeated upside surprises and cash returns.

Counter-risk
The decline may be profit-taking. Advanced packaging and high-bandwidth memory could lift earnings, while export rules, customer concentration, and cyclical spending remain risks.

Sources: Company release · Investor relations · AP · EDGAR

06 · COMPANY / PASSIVE FLOWS

Reddit will enter the S&P 500; shares jumped 12.6%

Fact
Reddit will join the S&P 500 before the August 18 open. Shares gained 12.6% Friday.

Market impact · Inference
The move mainly reflects expected passive demand and liquidity, not a same-day operating change.

Counter-risk
Mechanical buying does not imply durable returns; the event premium may fade after inclusion.

Sources: AP · Official S&P 500 page · Company investor relations · AP market report
