Daily Finance | Oil Reclaims the Inflation Narrative Before CPI

August 11, 2026 | Cutoff: 08:00 ET / 12:00 UTC | Tuesday premarket edition

Today's Through-Line

Brent crude rose another 2.4% to $89.80 a barrel after Monday's 5% jump, while global equities turned cautious before the U.S. July CPI release. Fact: the energy risk premium increased, Australia's central bank held rates, and Asian equities diverged. Inference: investors reduced exposure to both inflation surprises and geopolitical tail risk. View: tomorrow's core CPI components matter more than the 3.4% headline consensus because today's oil move affects future inflation data, not the full July sample due tomorrow.

1. Oil Rises for a Second Day as Hormuz Remains the Cross-Asset Pivot

  • Fact: At the cutoff, Brent was up 2.4% at $89.80 and WTI was up 2.6% at $84.28. Brent had already gained 5% on Monday and traded between $72 and $102 during the past month as expectations for reopening the Strait of Hormuz shifted.
  • Market impact — inference: Higher oil first lifts near-term inflation compensation and relative returns for energy shares while squeezing airlines, transport companies, and energy-importing economies. It also weakens the trade that soft employment will keep the Federal Reserve from raising rates.
  • Counter-risk: A diplomatic agreement, escort arrangement, or inventory release could quickly compress the risk premium; futures prices do not prove physical disruption. Conversely, deteriorating insurance costs and tanker traffic could mean the current move understates the real-economy shock.
  • Sources: AP: August 11 global markets and oil · U.S. EIA: world oil transit chokepoints · U.S. Maritime Administration: Gulf and Hormuz advisory · ICE: Brent crude futures

2. U.S. CPI Arrives Tomorrow: 3.4% Is a Forecast, Not a Release

  • Fact: The Bureau of Labor Statistics confirms that July CPI is due August 12 at 08:30 ET. The economist consensus cited by AP is 3.4% year over year, down from 3.5% in June. June CPI fell 0.4% month over month; core CPI was flat and up 2.6% year over year. July payrolls fell by 23,000, creating conflicting growth and inflation signals.
  • Market impact — inference: Benign core services and shelter readings could reinforce support for bonds and long-duration equities. A hot core print would combine with rising oil to lift the tail probability of a rate increase, potentially supporting the dollar and short-term yields.
  • Counter-risk: One CPI report can be distorted by energy, base effects, and seasonal adjustment. Even a lower headline rate would not mean the new oil shock has been absorbed. PPI on August 13 and retail sales on August 14 remain important cross-checks.
  • Sources: BLS: CPI homepage and release time · BLS: August release calendar · BLS: July employment report · AP: inflation and retail-sales week ahead

3. The Reserve Bank of Australia Holds at 4.35%; a Pause Is Not Easing

  • Fact: The Monetary Policy Board kept the cash-rate target at 4.35%. Australia's S&P/ASX 200 rose 0.2% to 9,250.60 after the decision. The central bank had raised rates three times in 2026, and its June meeting also unanimously held the rate at 4.35%.
  • Market impact — inference: The pause gives housing and rate-sensitive sectors limited breathing room, but 4.35% still represents restrictive financial conditions. For the Australian dollar, yield support competes with global risk aversion and the oil shock.
  • Counter-risk: One hold does not establish a cycle peak. Persistent inflation above the 2%–3% target could return another increase to the agenda; alternatively, faster weakness in housing and employment could expose excessive hawkish pricing.
  • Sources: Reserve Bank of Australia: cash-rate overview · Reserve Bank of Australia: 2026 decisions · Reserve Bank of Australia: June minutes · AP: Australian market after the decision

4. Asian Equities Split: Korean Chips Rebound as Chinese Markets Retreat

  • Fact: The Kospi gained 0.7% to 6,345.53; Samsung Electronics rose 4.1% and SK Hynix added 0.4%. Hong Kong's Hang Seng fell 1.1% to 25,652.82, the Shanghai Composite lost 0.8% to 3,934.09, Taiwan's Taiex rose 0.4%, and Tokyo was closed for a holiday.
  • Market impact — inference: The Korean memory rebound looks more like selective repair after extreme volatility than a synchronized improvement in Asian risk appetite. Chinese weakness and softer early European trading suggest investors are reducing directional exposure before CPI.
  • Counter-risk: Holidays, index weights, and one-day flows exaggerate regional differences. Samsung's one-day gain does not remove profitability risks from capacity expansion, capital spending, and Chinese competition.
  • Sources: AP: Asian market snapshot · Korea Exchange: market data · Hang Seng Indexes: index information · Shanghai Stock Exchange: index data · AP: Samsung's second-quarter results and capacity risks

5. Yen Intervention Fades as Dollar-Yen Returns to 159.35

  • Fact: The dollar traded near 159.35 yen, up from 159.30 in the prior session. Dollar-yen had fallen near 155.20 on August 3 after the United States and Japan confirmed joint intervention. The Bank of Japan's policy rate is 1%, versus the Federal Reserve's 3.50%–3.75% target range.
  • Market impact — inference: The return toward 160 suggests intervention changed the level but did not remove the structural pressure from the rate gap and Japan's energy-import bill. Higher oil plus a weaker yen increases imported-inflation risk.
  • Counter-risk: No official has declared 159 or 160 a defense line, and renewed intervention can create a fast reversal. A benign U.S. CPI reading that lowers Treasury yields could also cool the carry trade without official action.
  • Sources: AP: August 11 currency snapshot · AP: background on joint intervention · Bank of Japan: foreign-exchange data · U.S. Treasury: daily yield curve

6. Intel's Potential $15 Billion Raise Tests AI Dilution Risk

  • Fact: Intel said it may sell $15 billion of stock and would likely direct the proceeds toward heavy investment related to artificial intelligence technology. Its shares fell 4.1% on Monday. This is a potential financing plan, not a completed offering.
  • Market impact — inference: Investors are decomposing AI infrastructure into funding cost, per-share dilution, and project returns. The valuation gap may widen between companies that self-fund and those relying on new equity or expensive debt.
  • Counter-risk: New capital can strengthen capacity and the balance sheet; short-term dilution need not destroy long-term value when returns exceed the cost of capital. Size, timing, and use of proceeds may still change.
  • Sources: AP: Intel financing plan and market reaction · Intel: SEC filings · SEC: Intel company filings · Intel: investor relations

7. Earnings Growth Is Strong; AI Cloud Cash Flow Is the Next Test

  • Fact: S&P 500 second-quarter earnings per share are on track to rise 50% year over year, the fastest growth in five years, according to FactSet data cited by AP. CoreWeave reports second-quarter results after the U.S. close on August 11. Consensus estimates are not company guidance, and the result falls beyond this briefing's cutoff.
  • Market impact — inference: Strong index earnings cushion high valuations, but CoreWeave directly tests whether AI-compute rental growth can cover capital spending, financing costs, and customer concentration.
  • Counter-risk: The 50% figure is affected by base effects and large index weights and does not describe the median company. One cloud-compute operator should not be extrapolated across the entire AI supply chain.
  • Sources: AP: S&P 500 earnings-growth estimate · CoreWeave: investor relations · CoreWeave: quarterly results · Weekly earnings calendar · SEC: company filings search
