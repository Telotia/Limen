01

Make the subject almost disappear

Negative space as pressure

The head is pressed low and left. A bank-like plane falls down and right while the gaze rises up and left. One force sinks; the other lifts. Tension hangs between them.

Gaze risesPlane falls

Muted ochre, smoke, and charcoal form an analogous harmony. There is no clear light source, only value rubbed open. The warm palette feels depleted and cold.

02

The wall’s scars keep speaking

Surface carries history

Fact: one of Goya’s late Black Paintings, this work was made around 1820–1823 on a wall in his home, using a mixed mural technique, then transferred to canvas. Uneven paint and rubbed passages make the surface feel like lingering psychological weather.

Its familiar titles came later. Water, earth, or some other setting: the scene remains unresolved.

Hope shrinks to one upward tilt of the head; the world offers almost no reply.

My reading: danger is never stated, yet we have already begun searching for an exit.

03

Use scale contrast, not decoration

A practical ochre formula
  1. 70% sand ochre #B6975F for the field and largest garment.
  2. 20% smoke #6E5638 + 8% char #292820 for depth; reserve the darkest value for the smallest subject.
  3. 2% bone #DDD0AD for a collar, headline, or single reflection.
  4. Outfit: camel coat + smoke-brown knit + charcoal shoes and bag + bone collar.
