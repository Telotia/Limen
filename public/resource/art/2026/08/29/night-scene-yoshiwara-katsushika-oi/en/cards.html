Ink black holds nearly half the frame. Lantern parchment stays local; muted vermilion and indigo supply cool–warm tension. Value builds the drama, hue adds temperature.

HAND-PAINTED UKIYO-E · LAYERED INK · PAPER GRADATION

Direct painting lets black deepen unevenly and warm color dissolve softly. Fine lines preserve faces, patterned robes, and ornaments behind the grille. Light is carried by parchment, ochre, and vermilion—not empty white.

Fact: The work dates to about 1844–1854 and belongs to the Ōta Memorial Museum of Art. Katsushika Ōi was Hokusai’s daughter and long-term collaborator; only about ten works by her are confirmed.

Vision crosses the bars.
Bodies do not.

Interpretation: Customers, women inside, and the viewer form three layers of looking. Light creates beauty while exposing commerce, distance, and constraint. A Daily Art reading.
