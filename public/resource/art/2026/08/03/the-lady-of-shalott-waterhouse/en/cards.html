The Lady of Shalott

Not simply leaving—stepping out of the mirror
John William Waterhouse · 1888 · Oil on canvas
01

The boat is a moving frame

Composition · Colour · Light

The boat hugs the lower edge, placing us at water level. The figure travels from lower left toward centre-right; face, hands, and prow form a slow forward line. Reeds and trees tighten the space; the distant river leaves an exit.

Moss green, blue-grey, and umber hold the autumn dusk in analogous harmony. Lantern red breaks it as the complementary pulse. The pale dress lifts the figure by value contrast.

02

Light is fragile; the ending is not

Technique · Background · Why It Moves

Diffuse sky light opens the landscape; candle and lantern add two fragile points of warmth. Polished handling describes cloth, wood, and skin, while broken brushwork across water, reeds, and leaves turns observation into fable.

Waterhouse (1849–1917), born in Italy and active in Britain, worked within the Pre-Raphaelite orbit. The painting responds to Tennyson’s 1832 poem: she leaves the mirror that showed her only other people’s lives, choosing the world.
