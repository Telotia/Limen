Shift the center, and time begins

Off-center orbit · chromatic pulse

The navy core sits high and right; outer rings are cropped on every side. A static target becomes a moon shadow crossing the sky. Broken marks make the eye complete each orbit, turning vision into rotation.

Blue-orange complements create vibration. Midnight navy absorbs. White seams are not empty—they are the air that lets every color glow.

It looks improvised. It starts with a map.

A hand-painted system

Museum research found pencil underdrawings, ruled guides, and color notes beneath related paintings. Acrylic made pure hues flare against white. Small changes in width, spacing, and angle rescue the system from repetition.

Fact: Thomas devoted herself fully to painting after retirement. This final work in her Space series responded to the March 7, 1970 total solar eclipse. In 1972, she became the first Black woman to receive a solo show at the Whitney Museum of American Art.

Darkness is not color's absence. It gives every surrounding hue new force.

Bring the corona formula home

Color and layout recipe
  1. Layout: center at 68% left / 38% top; crop the outer rings.
  2. Palette: 35% yellow/orange + 25% coral + 20% blue-green + 20% white/navy.
  3. Styling: midnight coat + canvas-white base + corona-orange bag or shoes.
  4. Texture: draft tracks, then vary unit width and spacing by 10–20%.
