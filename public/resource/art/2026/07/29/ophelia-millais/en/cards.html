Ophelia

A flower, a river, and youth in suspension
John Everett Millais · 1851–1852 · Oil on canvas
01

A river with no exit

Composition · Colour

Ophelia lies along the lower edge, her body a slow horizontal line. Dense branches fracture the sky and offer no distant opening, so the eye travels with the water.

Deep foliage, olive and grey-blue make a cool field; russet gold supplies warmth. The red poppy forms a complementary contrast against green—a tiny wound rather than decoration.

02

Nature made into fate

Technique · Background · Why It Moves

Millais worked beside Surrey’s Hogsmill River for roughly five months before completing the figure in the studio. Plants, water and flowers receive specimen-like attention.

Even outdoor light lets the face and sleeves rise from the water. Elizabeth Siddal posed for the figure in a water-filled bathtub, holding the stillness of the scene in her body.

03

Let one small object carry the feeling

Borrow the Feeling · Source
  • 60% deep green + 20% grey-blue + 12% russet gold.
  • 5% bone white to lift the figure; 3% poppy red as the emotional mark.
  • Use a calm horizontal line, then interrupt it with a sinking body.
