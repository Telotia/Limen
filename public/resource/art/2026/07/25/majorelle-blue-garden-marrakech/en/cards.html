Majorelle Blue

The color a painter patented to keep
Jacques Majorelle · Marrakech, Morocco
01

Composition

Composition

A wall of cacti blocks the foreground — you have to look through the gaps to find the blue villa behind it, like stumbling into a private garden.

Cactus spines, the villa's boxy form, and palm trunks all rhyme vertically; only the railing and pool edge pin it down horizontally.

Color Theory

Color Theory

Majorelle Blue leans violet; the lemon curtain is nearly its complement — but yellow stays small-scale, so the contrast seasons rather than shouts. Mint colonnades buffer the two.

Technique

Technique

Cubist architect Paul Sinoir designed the villa in 1931; around 1937 Majorelle mixed and patented this deep blue himself.

Inspired by Marrakech's colored tiles and indigo Berber cloaks. Desert sun bleaches it, so the walls are repainted every year.

Why It Moves Us

Why It Moves Us

Spiny, austere desert cacti planted inside a garden colored like a carnival — harsh botany and maximalist color make each other work.

Nearly demolished for a hotel in 1980, Yves Saint Laurent bought and restored it; his ashes were scattered here in 2008.

05

Background

Background

Majorelle bought the land in 1923 and built the garden over nearly forty years. Sold off late in life, nearly lost, then saved by Saint Laurent and Bergé — it now draws 700,000+ visitors a year.

Steal This

Steal This
  • Deep violet-blue + lemon yellow: keep yellow to small accents, let blue own the large surfaces.
  • Slot a mint buffer between two strong contrasting colors for a smoother transition.
  • Ground a saturated color field with enough greenery to keep it vivid, not garish.
