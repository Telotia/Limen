KÄMPFENDE FORMEN · 1914

When color
starts to wrestle

Red bursts outward. Blue tightens inward. The most tense shape is the seam that keeps them apart.

01

One side expands.
The other contracts.

Centrifugal points × centripetal arcs × orange-blue
Form

Red-orange radiates through points; blue-black coils through arcs. A narrow S-shaped seam locks both actions together.

Color

Vermilion faces deep blue-teal. Similar area, different weight. Yellow advances; gray green and magenta mediate.

Light

No natural source: high-value warmth advances, low-value coolness recedes, and white scumbles pull the surface forward.

02

Conflict stays in
the present tense.

Color fields · layering and abrasion · crossing curves

Marc establishes two broad chromatic forces, then cuts into them with arcs, wedges, and thin dark lines. Scumbling, coverage, and translucent overlaps preserve earlier decisions, so the forms keep forming.

FACT: Painted in 1914. Marc was a German Expressionist and a central figure around the Blue Rider group. The collection does not define this as a specific war image.

Visual reading: There is no winner.
Every curve is intercepted by another.

Bodies almost disappear. Expansion and contraction, heat and cold, attack and defense remain. Looking becomes a physical contest.

03

Make two colors
perform two actions.

Palette · Layout · Outfit · Painting
  1. Palette: #D64A2F 35% + #103B58 / #17242C 30% + #6F9577 20% + #E9B73D 10% + #B97891 5%.
  2. Layout: Build a 55:45 confrontation: radiating points versus inward arcs. Place the narrowest negative space near center.
  3. Outfit: Deep blue 60% + vermilion 30% + mustard 10%. Pair one matte surface with one reflective surface.
  4. Painting: Start with two large shapes that perform different actions. Add only three to five curves across the boundary.
