One vast arc

Bottom-heavy · dense left · diagonal pressure · vertical reply
Composition

The hem sweeps across the lower frame. A leaning diagonal advances; vertical pleats and a lifted hand resist.

Color

Achromatic is not neutral: about 64% paper to 36% ink. One black mass sets weight; dots and lines set rhythm.

Light

There is no single source. Faces stay paper-white; the skirt becomes silhouette. Value serves drama, not nature.

02

A line already
thinking about print

Graphite placement · ink contour · solid fill · stipple · whiplash

Solid black, hairline contour, stipple, and blank paper act as four voices. Curves in hair, feathers, and hem accelerate, then tighten; ornament seems to pull the bodies.

Fact: The 1893 ink-and-graphite drawing belongs to the Salomé series. Museum sources connect it to Japanese prints, peacock imagery, and early British Art Nouveau. The artist's career lasted under seven years; he died at 25.

Reading: two faces nearly touch,
yet their gazes never meet.

Costume is seduction and armor. Black erases bodily detail, enlarging one person into an unavoidable graphic force.

03

Bring “one vast shape + space”
into layout and dress

Composition · monochrome · one accent · silhouette
  1. Frame: a dark arc fills 35–45% below; place the face on an upper-third point and leave 25% open opposite.
  2. Value: #F2EFE6 64% + #11100E 36%. Build one large black mass before adding detail.
  3. Accent: paper 70% + ink 25% + peacock green #176A62 5%; use green only for folios or one word.
  4. Styling: black long layer 60% + ivory base 35% + green accessory 5%. Choose silhouette over trinkets.
