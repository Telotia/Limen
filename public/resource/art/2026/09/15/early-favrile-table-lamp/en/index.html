DAILY ART · 2026.09.15COLLECTION FILE 01 / A VESSEL FOR LIGHT
TABLE LAMP · NEW YORK · CA. 1895–98

Making night
into a lamp

Light is not cast onto the work; light is the work. Metal blocks, glass releases.

01

An umbrella steadies luminous fruit

COMPOSITION

The triangle orders; the circle breathes

A broad conical shade presses down on a swollen glass reservoir like architecture compressed to a tabletop. A strong axis locks the isosceles triangle above to the round font and scrolling feet below. The shade is deliberately top-heavy, yet beads dissolve its rim into points and the feet distribute weight outward. Mass seems to hover.

Radial ribs open from the crown. Long oval panes alternate with spiral ornaments; chains add another tempo beneath the edge. From across a room, it is a decisive silhouette. Up close, it becomes a score of lines, points, ovals, and coils.

Fact: the collection dates it to about 1895–98 and describes leaded Favrile glass with patinated bronze. It precedes the floral leaded-glass shades that later became widely familiar.
02

One warm hue, several material states

PALETTE / COLOR THEORY

Low-chroma warmth, awakened by teal

This is not one “gold.” Near-black bronze and deep patina move into caramel amber, wick yellow, and milky glass. Tiny peacock-teal jewels near the feet form a muted near-complement to orange and keep the warmth from turning sugary.

03

Metal blocks; glass releases

LIGHT / TECHNIQUE

The brightest places sit inside the structure

Light leaks through long oval panes and is cut into narrow radial bands by dark ribs. The structure tightens at the crown and opens at the rim, so brightness moves from concentration toward diffusion. Below, the milky font is softer, as if leftover light had settled inside translucent fruit.

The shade organizes leaded panes into a dome; the oil font is blown Favrile glass, retaining cloudiness, striations, and interior glow. Favrile is an art glass associated with an iridescent surface. Its name was patented in 1894 and is related to an older word for hand-wrought making.

Interpretation: the denser the brown-black metal becomes, the more alive the amber appears. The lamp lets us see light confined and escaping at once.
04

Before the later garden of flowers

MAKER / WHY IT MOVES

An invention still in progress

The collection credits Tiffany Studios and calls this one of its earliest lamps. Its floral leaded-glass shades had not yet become the familiar signature, and experiments with blown art glass were still recent. The museum also identifies Islamic-inspired pattern on the bronze collar and links the paisley-shaped enamel jewels on the feet to an early-1890s interior project.

The collection does not identify an individual designer for this lamp, so this essay does not assign one. Interpretation: opulence remains quiet because every ornament measures light. Beads describe gravity, ribs describe diffusion, and cloudy glass describes storage.

05

Borrow its amber formula

PRACTICAL

Control hue; multiply material
