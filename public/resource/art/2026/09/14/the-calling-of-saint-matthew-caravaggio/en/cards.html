The horizontal holds routine.
The diagonal breaks it.

LOW GROUP · DIAGONAL BEAM · LOW-VALUE WARMTH · TENEBRISM
Composition

Figures sit low; the table builds a stable horizontal. Light and gesture share one upper-right-to-lower-left vector.

Color

Charcoal, smoke brown, and umber lead. Muted oxblood and olive meet; ivory is rationed to faces, hands, and linen.

Light

Darkness becomes a pause. The beam selects eyes, fingers, and coins, turning the call into a visible route.

OIL ON CANVAS · FROM LIFE · CONTEMPORARY DRESS · SELECTIVE LIGHT

Taut highlights and rapidly vanishing edges make the scene feel caught, not arranged. Caravaggio brings ordinary faces and contemporary clothes into sacred history.

Fact: A 1599 contract commissioned two lateral paintings for the Contarelli Chapel; they were installed in 1600. Matthew’s visual identity remains debated.

Interpretation: real change
contains half a second of doubt.

One man has turned, one has just understood, and one still counts. The painting completes no decision for us.

COMPOSITION · PALETTE · PHOTOGRAPHY · LAYOUT
  1. Composition: Keep the group within the lower 45–55%; leave 30–40% dark space above. Run a 20–30° diagonal through eyes and hands.
  2. Palette: #171713 45% + #3B3025 25% + #6C5A3B 15% + #D6C39A 10%; keep oxblood and olive below 3% each.
  3. Photography: Hold the background 2–3 stops below highlights. Light faces, hands, and one narrative prop; let other edges disappear.
  4. Layout: Keep type horizontal and use one diagonal for direction. Let a static order change course once.
