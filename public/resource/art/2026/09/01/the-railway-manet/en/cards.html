REPEATED VERTICALS · TWIN TRIANGLES · DIFFUSE LIGHT

The woman meets us; the girl turns away. A raised arm bridges them. Iron bars divide the width into narrow beats while two skirts spread into broad triangles—stability and obstruction at once.

Blue edged in white; white tied with blue. The colors reverse roles. Navy and steam white build value contrast; tiny poppy red meets muted green as a restrained complement and first focal point.

BROAD STROKES · FLAT DEPTH · ABSENT SUBJECT

Blue-gray, cream, and dry pale strokes remain visible on the white dress. The fence is heavy yet slightly unsteady. Manet preserves the speed of something just seen.

Fact: painted in 1873, oil on canvas, 93.3 × 111.5 cm. The sitter is Victorine Meurent; part of the Pont de l’Europe appears at upper right. Manet made it first in his nearby new studio and showed it at the 1874 Salon.

The title promises a railway.
The image gives steam, bars, attention.

Interpretation: this is no simple hymn to machinery. The adult’s gaze reaches us; the child’s curiosity crosses the bars. Intimacy and isolation happen in the same second.
